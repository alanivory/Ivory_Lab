Urban stormwater sewer systems are vast, interconnected networks beneath our cities — and they turn out to be home to a surprising array of wildlife. This project documents vertebrate diversity in the stormwater sewers of Alachua County, Florida, including alligators (*Alligator mississippiensis*), raccoons (*Procyon lotor*), Southeastern Myotis bats (*Myotis austroriparius*), and many other species.

## Research Questions

- What vertebrate species use stormwater sewer systems, and how frequently?
- What environmental and structural factors predict wildlife use of this infrastructure?
- What are the implications for urban wildlife management and infrastructure design?

## Methods

Camera traps, acoustic monitoring, and systematic surveys were used to detect and identify species across a network of stormwater access points. Data were analyzed using occupancy modeling and generalized linear mixed models in R.
