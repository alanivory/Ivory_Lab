Cogongrass (*Imperata cylindrica*) is one of the world's most damaging invasive plants and is rapidly spreading across the southeastern United States. Its dense, highly flammable biomass fundamentally alters the fire environment in invaded ecosystems — with consequences for wildfire risk, native community composition, and ecosystem services.

## Research Questions

- How does cogongrass invasion affect fire intensity, spread rate, and behavior across environmental and seasonal gradients?
- How does cogongrass alter plant community composition and ecosystem services in southeastern forests?
- What spatiotemporal patterns characterize cogongrass invasion, and what landscape factors predict spread?

## Methods

Field measurements of fuel loads, fire behavior observations, remote sensing (LiDAR and multispectral imagery), and fire behavior modeling are combined to predict cogongrass impacts across the landscape. Statistical analyses are performed in R using linear models and spatial analysis tools in ArcGIS Pro.
